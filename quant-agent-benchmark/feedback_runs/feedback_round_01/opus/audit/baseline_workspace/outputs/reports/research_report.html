Why this model. the bootstrap reprices its pillars exactly but its instantaneous forward curve runs from -1.76% to 5.85% against a quoted range of 1.21%-2.58%, breaching the admissibility band by 1.28 percentage points; that is exact interpolation of quote noise between nearly coincident pillars, not information, and it makes forward-starting valuation and key-rate risk unusable. The penalised spline is admissible, so it is selected (holdout weighted RMSE: bootstrap 0.71bp, penalised spline 1.15bp)

What the data required. 15 observations were repaired (unit rescaling, quotes reconstructed from the two-way market, crossed markets un-crossed), 14 were downweighted for width or illiquidity and 24 were excluded (stale snapshots, superseded duplicates and gross outliers). Every decision is recorded row by row in diagnostics/cleaning.csv.

How much to trust it. Weighted repricing RMSE, in yield-equivalent basis points, is 0.09bp for the deposits (n=16), 0.89bp for the OIS swaps (n=59), 2.55bp for the bonds (n=44); the bond figure is the same size as the bonds' own measured idiosyncratic spread and is a property of the instruments rather than an error in the curve. Discount factors are strictly positive across the whole grid. The sensitivity suite below shows the largest control-driven shift is 7.50 bp; the difference between the two candidate estimators reaches 7.50 bp, which is the honest width of the modelling choice.

2. Methodology

Conventions

Every convention stated in the supplied CONVENTIONS.md is followed exactly, and nothing beyond it is assumed. Zero rates are continuously compounded with D(T)=exp(-zT); deposits use simple interest D(T)=1/(1+rT); OIS fixed legs pay annually through 2Y and semiannually thereafter with accrual 1/frequency; bonds pay level coupons at 1/frequency intervals on face 100 with no accrued interest. The document is silent on one point that matters: how many periods a non-integer maturity has. Schedules are generated backwards from maturity using n = round(T x frequency), and that rule was inferred from the quotes rather than assumed — it is the only one that simultaneously reproduces the 1.25Y annual-pay OIS quote (one period) and the 2.44Y semiannual bond quote (five periods). ceil misprices the first by roughly 50 bp and floor misprices the second by roughly 60 bp.

Curve representation

Both estimators are defined through the instantaneous forward rate and discount factors follow as D(T)=exp(-∫f). That construction makes negative zero and forward rates perfectly representable while discount factors remain strictly positive by construction — the requirement is satisfied structurally rather than by clipping. Outside the calibrated maturity range the forward is held flat, which is the standard extrapolation and the only one that adds no unsupported curvature.

Baseline: bootstrap_loglinear_df

sequential bootstrap of consensus pillars; log-linear discount factors (piecewise-constant instantaneous forwards); every pillar repriced exactly. Economically identical quotes are averaged into one consensus pillar with the calibration weights; pillars closer than 0.10Y are merged because an exact bootstrap cannot support two nearly coincident maturities. 58 pillars were used.

Advanced: penalised_robust_forward_spline

cubic spline in the instantaneous forward rate, fitted by weighted non-linear least squares with a second-derivative roughness penalty and Tukey-biweight iteratively reweighted least squares; flat-forward extrapolation outside the calibrated maturity range. The objective is a weighted sum of squared yield-equivalent repricing residuals plus λ∫(T/1Y)p f''(T)2 dT. The maturity-dependent factor matters: an unweighted roughness penalty over-penalises the money-market end, where curvature naturally scales like 1/T2 and where the data are densest. Both λ and p are selected by maturity-blocked cross-validation on the training sample only, so the holdout number reported below stays honest. The selected values are λ=1e-05 and p=2 on 21 knots placed at the benchmark rate maturities.

Weights and robustness

Each quote carries an uncertainty σi2 = σquote,i2 + σmodel,type2. The first term is the half bid/ask width converted to yield-equivalent basis points and inflated by illiquidity; the second is estimated from the residual dispersion of a preliminary robust fit, separately per instrument type. That second term is what keeps coupon bonds in their place: their two-way markets are tight, but no single OIS-consistent discount curve can absorb their idiosyncratic spreads, and the estimated model error (bond: 2.14 bp, deposit: 0.25 bp, ois_swap: 0.56 bp) says so quantitatively. Data-quality penalties multiply the weight for repaired, crossed, wide or illiquid quotes. Robustness is then iterative: a convex Huber warm-up (which cannot settle on the wrong cluster of quotes) followed by Tukey biweight reweighting with a frozen robust scale.

What was done about it

  • Unit errors. A quote is rescaled by a power of 100 only when that rescaling moves it an order of magnitude closer to a robust reference built from its nearest maturity peers of the same type. Using a local reference rather than a whole-sample one is deliberate: on a steep curve a 1M rate can legitimately sit two orders of magnitude below a 30Y rate.
  • Missing quotes. Reconstructed from the bid/ask mid where a two-way market exists, and excluded otherwise.
  • Crossed markets. Bid and ask are swapped and the observation is penalised, not discarded — the mid is still informative.
  • Stale snapshots. Staleness is measured relative to the most recent timestamp in the file rather than against the valuation date, so a uniformly time-shifted data set is not wiped out.
  • Duplicates. Ranked by freshness, then source, then spread; the survivor is named in the audit trail of every superseded row.
  • Gross outliers. Flagged against a deliberately flexible robust reference fit, and measured against each quote's own pillar consensus (a shorth, which a contaminated minority cannot move) rather than against the fitted curve. Three 7Y swaps that agree with each other to a third of a basis point are not three bad prints, however far a smoothed curve sits from them. Exclusion needs two conditions: more than 4.7 robust sigma and at least 5bp from those neighbours in yield-equivalent terms. The second gate is not decoration — a flexible reference drives the robust scale below half a basis point, and without an absolute floor a quote 2bp from its peers reads as a forty-sigma event. No maturity neighbourhood is ever emptied completely, and at most a quarter of the sample can be removed.

4. Baseline versus advanced

Holdout design. maturity-blocked interpolation holdout: 44 blocks formed with a max(gap 0.15Y, 2% of maturity) rule, every 4th interior block withheld. A random split would be worthless here: four venues quote the same 10Y pillar and several bonds mature within days of a benchmark swap, so a random split leaks near-identical instruments across train and validation and measures quote dispersion instead of curve quality. Blocks move as a unit, and the first and last blocks are never withheld so the training set always spans the full maturity range — the metric therefore measures interpolation, not extrapolation policy. 100 instruments trained, 19 withheld across 10 blocks.

Selection rule (fixed before the numbers were looked at). 1) reject any curve whose instantaneous forward leaves the quoted rate range by more than the admissibility tolerance; 2) among admissible curves take the lower maturity-blocked holdout weighted RMSE, requiring the advanced estimator to beat the baseline by the parsimony margin before its extra complexity is accepted.

The honest reading of the table is that the bootstrap is not beaten on repricing. It reprices its own pillars essentially exactly, and on the blocked holdout it is competitive. What disqualifies it is the shape of what it produces between those pillars: its instantaneous forward runs from -1.76% to 5.85% against a quoted rate range of 1.21%-2.58%. Those excursions are not information; they are the arithmetic consequence of forcing a curve through two pillars a few weeks apart whose quotes differ by a basis point of idiosyncratic noise. A curve carrying them cannot price a forward-starting trade and produces a key-rate profile that jumps with the pillar set. Forward-curve roughness differs by 35,996x between the two.

Risk

DV01 is the central finite difference of the receiver / fixed-instrument PV for a parallel one-basis-point move of the zero curve, on notional 1,000,000 for deposits and swaps and face 100 for bonds. Key rates use triangular tent bumps centred on 2Y, 5Y, 10Y and 30Y that peak at their own tenor, decay linearly to zero at the neighbouring tenors and are flat outside the range, so the four shapes sum to exactly one at every maturity.

  • Key-rate sensitivities add back to the parallel DV01 to within 5.32e-07 relative across all 119 instruments.
  • The finite-difference DV01 agrees with the closed-form cash-flow derivative to within 1.27e-06 relative, which is the expected truncation error of a 1 bp central difference. This check is what caught a sign error in the float-leg derivative during development.

8. Limitations and model risk

  • Single-curve assumption. One discount curve is fitted to deposits, OIS and bonds together. In a real market these are three funding bases; here the bond basis is absorbed into an estimated per-type model error (2.14 bp) rather than modelled explicitly. Bond-specific valuation should not use this curve without an asset-swap spread.
  • Extrapolation. Beyond the longest calibrating instrument the instantaneous forward is held flat. That is a policy, not a measurement, and it carries no market information.
  • Front end. Below the shortest deposit the forward is likewise flat. Nothing in the data constrains overnight-to-1M shape.
  • Schedule convention. The round(T x frequency) period count is inferred from the supplied quotes, not documented explicitly. Half-integer T x frequency cases do not occur in this data set, and the code rounds half away from zero; a conforming file that used banker's rounding there would disagree.
  • Holdout scope. The blocked holdout tests interpolation across withheld maturity regions. It cannot test extrapolation, and it cannot distinguish a genuinely local term-structure feature from noise.
  • Idiosyncratic bond noise is irreducible. Roughly 2.1 bp of bond repricing error is not a fitting failure and cannot be removed by any smooth single curve.
  • No uncertainty band is published. The sensitivity suite brackets the answer but is not a posterior; the numbers in curves/curve.csv are point estimates.

The full treatment, including numerical failure modes and the conditions under which each default stops being appropriate, is in MODEL_RISKS.md.

9. Recommended next steps

  1. Fit an explicit bond asset-swap spread curve on top of the OIS discount curve instead of absorbing the basis into a per-type error term; that would let bonds inform the long end without contaminating it.
  2. Publish an uncertainty band by bootstrapping the calibration residuals, so downstream users get a distribution rather than a point curve.
  3. Add forward-starting instruments (FRAs, forward swaps) to constrain the forward curve directly rather than inferring it from spot instruments.
  4. Track the estimated per-type model error over successive snapshots; a structural change in it is an early warning that the single-curve assumption is breaking down.
  5. Extend the outlier screen with a same-instrument time-series check once more than one snapshot is available; the current screen is purely cross-sectional.
