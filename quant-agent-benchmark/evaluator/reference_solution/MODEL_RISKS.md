# Model risks

The reference is a validation anchor, not a claim of unique correctness. Cubic interpolation can overshoot when observations are sparse; a curvature penalty and forward diagnostics reduce but do not eliminate that risk. Flat-zero extrapolation beyond the fitted grid is simple and stable but may be economically implausible under structural regime changes. Unit inference combines magnitude and spread evidence and can remain ambiguous near zero rates. Robust residual rules can downweight real local features, while wide-spread quotes receive less influence even when informative. Bond timing uses the synthetic ACT/365F schedule specified by the benchmark and is not a general holiday-calendar engine. Visible holdout results are not substitutes for temporal validation on live markets. Key-rate allocations are a partitioned approximation whose sum matches parallel DV01; local bump design should be reconsidered for hedging production portfolios.
