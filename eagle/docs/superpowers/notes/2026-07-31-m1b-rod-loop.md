# Wave 2 M1b — the P66 rate loop, 2026-07-31

Work on ledger open item 1 ("P66's rate loop"), from
`docs/superpowers/notes/2026-07-26-m1-pdi-flight.md`. Plan:
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-31-eagle-m1b-p66-rate-loop.md`.

**Headline: the shipped `TAUROD` b-scale rests on a premise this repo has
itself already disproved, so the AGC has been flying a rate loop 4× or 8×
too fast — but which of the two is not yet established, and the six
existing flight traces cannot settle it. Flight 7 was instrumented to
settle it by measurement.**

## 1. The defect in the shipped scale

`scenarios/p66-padload.toml` carries two comment blocks fifteen lines
apart that contradict each other.

Lines 252-259 derive the whole P66 cluster from a global premise:

> velocities in this file are at 2^10 m/cs (VIGN literal, DDUM denominator
> comment) […] `TAUROD = vel/acc -> fraction = tau_cs / 2^14` (b=14,
> CENTISECONDS)

Lines 265-274 — added **later**, from the spike-B live read-back, and the
only entry in `padload::P66_BSCALE_TABLE` marked `Verified` by
measurement — pin the two words that actually appear in TAUROD's divide:

> VDGVERT and HDOTDISP are DP b=7 in m/cs: HDOTDISP read back as
> `hi=0o36` = 491520 pulses while N63 R2 displayed +00756 (75.6 ft/s), and
> `491520 · 2^-21 m/cs` = 0.2344 m/cs = 76.9 ft/s

The rope's force law divides *those two words*, not `VBRFG`/`VIGN`
(`vendor/virtualagc/Luminary099/LUNAR_LANDING_GUIDANCE_EQUATIONS.agc:1041-1044`):

```
:1041  STODL  HCALC1
:1042           HDOTDISP
:1042  BDSU   DDV
:1043           VDGVERT
:1044           TAUROD
```

**TAUROD was never re-derived after the spike-B measurement landed.** The
b=14 word is 150 cs = 1.5 s only if the numerator is at b=10; at the
measured b=7 it is not. That much is certain regardless of what the right
answer turns out to be.

## 2. What the right scale is — NOT resolved

`b_TAUROD = b_vel - b_acc`, and `b_vel = 7` is measured. The acceleration
side is not pinned, because the path from TAUROD's quotient to a quantity
of known scale passes through two unresolved shifts, each worth exactly
one power of two:

- **`BDDV` by `22D`** (`:1051-1058`). `22D` is a direction cosine built by
  `DOT` of a `CDU*NBSM`-transformed `UNITX` with `14D`. The interpreter's
  `DOT` carries an inherent factor of two for unit vectors — note the
  explicit `SL1` immediately after the `DOT` at `:928` — so whether
  `/AFC/` and the pre-`BDDV` sum share a scale depends on a convention not
  established here.
- **`SR2`** on the gravity path (`:1047`), between `DDV GSCALE` and the
  `DAD` that adds the two terms at `:1050`.

Candidate answers and what the shipped 150 cs word decodes to:

| true b | AGC reads | factor vs intended 1.5 s |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | 0.1875 s | 8× too fast |
| 12 | 0.375 s | 4× too fast |
| 14 (shipped) | 1.5 s | correct |

Either error is a bang-bang oscillator against the rope's own 0.2 s engine
lag (`THROTLAG`, `CONTROLLED_CONSTANTS.agc:134`), which is the observed
symptom. **The direction of the error is certain; the factor is not.**

Anchors that *are* solid and should not be re-derived:

- `MASS` b=16 kg — `ERASABLE_ASSIGNMENTS.agc:1698`,
  "`# (1) MASS AFTER STAGING, SCALE AT B16 KG`".
- `GSCALE` = `2DEC 100 B-11` (`:1477`) — 100 cs at b=11, a DP time in
  centiseconds in the same velocity÷time→acceleration role.
- `/AFC/` at b=-4 m/cs² — `THROTTLE_CONTROL_ROUTINES.agc:206`,
  "MASSMULT SCALES ACCELERATION, ARRIVING IN A AND L IN UNITS OF
  2(-4) M/CS/CS"; `ABRFG` annotated `B+04`.

The gap is only *where in the chain* the b=-4 quantity sits relative to
TAUROD's quotient.

## 3. Why the existing traces cannot settle it

Two methods were tried against `build/traces/telem-m1-run{4,5,6}.jsonl`.
Both failed, and the failures are informative enough to record so nobody
repeats them.

### 3a. Differencing the throttle command against HDOTDISP

`rod_fit`'s original method. `VDGVERT` is not telemetered, but differencing
across a display repaint cancels it *and* the gravity term, leaving
`Δ(a_cmd·cosθ) = -(1/τ)·ΔHDOTDISP`.

Measured:

```
telem-m1-run4.jsonl: tau = 27.0425 s  (slope -0.0370 1/s, r2 0.153, n 36,  75 unsaturated repaints)
telem-m1-run5.jsonl: tau = 27.8745 s  (slope -0.0359 1/s, r2 0.047, n 107, 198 unsaturated repaints)
telem-m1-run6.jsonl: tau = 35.4682 s  (slope -0.0282 1/s, r2 0.040, n 101, 188 unsaturated repaints)
```

r² of 0.15 / 0.05 / 0.04 — **no fit.** The plan's kill criterion (r² ≥ 0.5)
was written before the numbers were seen, and it fired.

**Cause:** the method needs `VDGVERT` constant across each difference, and
`scenarios/pdi-descent.toml` drives a ROD *schedule* (run 4's
`[[245,-5.3],[50,-0.3],[12,0.7]]`). The sim is clicking `VDGVERT`
throughout the entire P66 window, so the cancellation never happens. A
direct look at run 4's repaint samples confirms it: between TIG+992.6 and
TIG+999.6 the command rises 4.2 → 31.0 kN while `HDOTDISP` also rises
−8.11 → −6.10 m/s, which the law forbids at constant `VDGVERT` — the error
term would be shrinking, not growing.

### 3b. Reading MINFORCE off the command floor — DISCARDED

The command floor looked like a `MINFORCE` clamp at half its loaded value:
run 4 bottoms at 178 bits = 2231 N against a pad-loaded 4560 N, and
`4560/2 = 2280 N = 182 bits` sits between run 4's minimum (178) and its 1st
percentile (187). That would have pinned the force words, and with
`b_force = b_MASS + b_acc` it would have pinned TAUROD without resolving
either shift above.

**It is not a clamp.** Histogramming run 4's low tail shows a smooth
distribution — about 10 frames (one guidance cycle at 10 Hz telemetry) at
each of ~40 consecutive bit values, 178 through 259, with no spike
anywhere:

```
  178 bits =  2230.7 N   10
  183 bits =  2293.3 N   10
  187 bits =  2343.5 N   10
  ...                    ...
  255 bits =  3195.6 N   21
  258 bits =  3233.2 N   20
```

A clamp produces a mode; this is a controller sweeping through a range.
Independently, `throttle_cmd_pulses` is our accumulation of the DINC
stream, and the rope's `PIFPSET` drive-past bookkeeping
(`THROTTLE_CONTROL_ROUTINES.agc:99-119`) offsets it from `FC`, so its
absolute level cannot measure a force word in any case.

**What the same data does establish:** runs 4 and 6 hit exactly 4096 bits
= `FEXTRA` (`THROTTLE_CONTROL_ROUTINES.agc:226`, `BIT13`, "4096 PULSES"),
the rope's own full-throttle drive-past constant. Landing on it exactly
validates the bit→newton mapping and the telemetry chain end to end.

## 4. What was built to settle it

- **`runtime/apps/eagle-runtime/src/bin/rod_fit.rs`** (`4c55466d`,
  extended `3eaed239`) — offline estimator, 5 tests.
- **`rod_clicks_cum` in the telemetry wire** (`24d81e56`) — the sim issues
  every ROD click, so it reports the running total and the analysis
  reconstructs `VDGVERT` exactly, at the live-verified 1 ft/s per click.
  Additive field; `SCHEMA_VERSION` stays 2. The handover's selection click
  is issued by `headless.rs`, not `phase8_rod`, and is booked from the
  same latch that triggers it.
- **Click-aware level regression** (`3eaed239`) — with
  `VDGVERT(t) = VDGVERT₀ + k·Δclicks`, the law is a straight line in the
  fully-observed regressor `x = k·Δclicks − HDOTDISP`, so `τ = 1/slope`
  and no constant-`VDGVERT` segment is needed. Recovers τ at 0.1875 /
  0.375 / 1.5 s to within 0.01 s *through* a `VDGVERT` change, so the
  candidate scales are separable.
- **`EAGLE_TELEM_OUT` / `EAGLE_ATT_DEBUG` fail loudly** (`259f6af2`) —
  both did `File::create(p).ok()`. The runtime's cwd is `runtime/` (the
  Makefile does `cd runtime && cargo run`), so the natural
  `EAGLE_TELEM_OUT=build/traces/x.jsonl` from the repo root resolves under
  `runtime/` to a directory that does not exist, and the instrumentation
  silently did nothing. **This cost a full 20-minute descent on
  2026-07-31** before it was noticed. Use absolute paths.

Comparable to run 6 (30.86 / 60.04 / 12.8°), as expected — nothing about

**The flight did NOT produce the measurement, and the reason is a new
blocker.** `HDOTDISP` froze 1.6 s into P66 and never moved again:

| run | P66 frames | duration | distinct `HDOTDISP` | repaints/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2264 | 226.3 s | 110 | 0.49 |
| 5 | 2100 | 209.9 s | 206 | 0.98 |
| 6 | 2163 | 216.2 s | 228 | 1.05 |
| **7** | **2220** | **222 s** | **6** | **0.02** |

Run 7's entire P66 display history is three values, all inside the first
1.6 s:

```
  t=  986.1s  hdot= -3.2918  alt=  244.9  pulses= 1141
  t=  986.8s  hdot= -2.9566  alt=  243.3  pulses= 1015
  t=  987.7s  hdot= -2.8651  alt=  241.2  pulses=  715
```

`agc_alt_m` froze with it (5 distinct values against run 5's 108), so it
is the whole flight display, not one register. The AGC itself kept flying
— the throttle command ranged 232 to 2495 bits for the remaining 216 s.

**Mechanism, from this repo's own documentation.** `runner.rs:236-243`
records that P66's `VERTDISP` repaints V06N60 every guidance pass
(`LUNAR_LANDING_GUIDANCE_EQUATIONS.agc:899-900`), and that a load typed
into that repaint stream "is rejected mid-sequence with OPR ERR and the
KEY REL lamp lit, leaving RODCOUNT unwritten and VDGVERT unmoved
(spike-B iter 18)". `rod_load` guards against this with
`grab_dsky` (KEY REL, then wait for the repaint burst to subside), but the
guard is best-effort — `wait_for_display_gap` gives up after 1.2 s.

The freeze timing matches the ROD loads exactly:

```
  click change ->  -1 at t=986.1s (alt 244.9)   <- handover selection click
  click change ->  -6 at t=988.2s (alt 239.9)   <- schedule batch
  display froze at t=987.7s
  ... 216 s with no display update, no further clicks ...
  click change ->   0 at t=1204.6s (alt 39.7)
  click change ->   2 at t=1205.7s (alt  9.1)
```

**Two consequences, and the second is worse than the first.**

1. The display is dead, so `agc_hdot_ms` is unusable and neither fitting
   method can run (`rod_fit` reports 3 unsaturated repaints, no fit).
2. **If a `rod_load` was swallowed, its clicks never reached RODCOUNT** —
   so `rod_clicks_cum` would report clicks the AGC never applied, and
   `VDGVERT` would not be where the count says. That breaks the click-aware
   fit's core assumption independently of the display. Flight 7 also
   counted **41 PROG lamp frames** after ignition, against 0 on runs 4
   and 6, which is consistent with something having gone wrong in the
   dialog — but the code is unknown for the same reason as ledger item 2:
   nothing reads FAILREG after ENGINE ON.

**This is not a regression from this session's changes.** The binary that
flew carried only `rod_fit` (a separate bin), the `rod_clicks_cum` counter
(`sim.rs` `tick`, a pure accumulator), and the env-var panics (startup
only) — none touches `rod_load`, the script pump, or the DSKY. Run 4's
0.49 repaints/s against runs 5 and 6's ~1.0 suggests the same failure
partially present earlier and never noticed.

**Next flight must capture `--trace-out`.** Run 7 did not, so the KEY REL
and OPR ERR lamp states — which would confirm or refute the swallow
directly — are not recoverable from it. That is the single cheapest thing
that would settle it.

## 5a. What to fix before flight 8

Ranked, and none of it costs a flight to attempt:

1. **Verify the ROD load landed.** `rod_load` writes RODCOUNT and returns
   without checking. A read-back races RODCOMP (which zeroes the word
   within one 1-second pass — `runner.rs:233-234`), but `VDGVERT` itself
   is stable and readable. Confirming the commanded rate actually moved
   would turn a silent failure into a loud one.
2. **Re-establish the flight display after a load.** If the swallow leaves
   KEY REL latched, one more KEY REL would restore VERTDISP. Today nothing
   does, so a single failure blinds every downstream measurement for the
   rest of the run.
3. **Capture `--trace-out` on every instrumented flight**, so lamp state is
   always recoverable after the fact.

**And the τ measurement still cannot be made from a descent.** This is not
an instrumentation problem, and no better estimator will fix it.

Three estimators were tried, all on good data:

| method | τ | r² |
|---|---|---|
| differencing across repaints | 12.94 s | 0.088 |
| click-aware level regression (PRIMARY) | 18.66 s | 0.042 |
| truth `vz` at 10 Hz, best command lag | 4.57 s | 0.63 |

The third looks like it worked. It did not. Sweeping the assumed command
lag shows r² is a **periodic function of the lag**, peaking and collapsing
on a fixed cycle:

```
 lag_s     tau_s      r2
   0.0    61.459   0.003
   2.0     6.434   0.297
   4.0     4.422   0.634
   5.0     4.396   0.641   <- peak
   6.0     4.818   0.534
   8.0     8.136   0.188
  10.0    65.859   0.003   <- collapse
  14.0       nan   0.184   <- rising again
```

The limit-cycle period, from `vz` zero crossings, is **19.4 s** — a
quarter period of **4.9 s**. The r² peak sits at **5.0 s**. A
quarter-period shift is exactly what maximizes the correlation between an
oscillation and the response driving it, and the collapse at 10 s is the
half-period anti-correlation. **The regression is measuring the limit
cycle's own geometry, not the control law**, and its "τ" tracks the period
rather than the gain — which is also why τ shrank monotonically as the
assumed lag grew.

**The root cause is structural: a saturated relay limit cycle carries
almost no information about the linear gain inside it.** The loop spends
its time at the stops, crosses the linear band quickly, and every signal
is dominated by phase. All three failures — §3a, the r²=0.04 level fit,
and this one — are the same fact seen three ways.

## 7. What will actually work: an open-loop step test

Stop trying to identify the loop from a descent. Do to `TAUROD` what
spike B did to `RODSCALE`: a short, controlled, live calibration.

`tests/live_spike_p66.rs` already boots to P66 at a benign state and reads
`cmd_pulses` (`:152`). The experiment:

1. Reach P66 and let the rate settle somewhere well inside the throttle
   band — no saturation at either stop.
2. Step `VDGVERT` by a known number of clicks (`rod_load`, now verified to
   land, at a live-confirmed 1 ft/s each).
3. Measure the resulting **step** in commanded thrust before the vehicle's
   own rate responds.

Then `τ = Δ(rate error) / Δ(a_cmd)` directly, from a step whose input is
exactly known and whose output is a single algebraic difference. One clean
step separates 0.1875 / 0.375 / 1.5 s by a factor of 4 to 8; no regression,
no phase, no saturation.

This costs roughly 10 minutes rather than a 20-minute descent, and it is
the same pattern this repo already trusts for `RODSCALE` — which is the
one b-scale in `P66_BSCALE_TABLE` marked `Verified` by measurement.
